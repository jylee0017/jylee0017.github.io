Systems neuroscience · Neural coding · Behavior

Understanding how the brain makes sense of sound.

I am a post-master's researcher in Dr. Gunsoo Kim's Auditory Processing Lab at the Korea Brain Research Institute (KBRI). My work examines how neurons represent vocalizations across changing acoustic environments, combining electrophysiological recordings with computational analyses.

I am especially interested in how neural populations encode behaviorally relevant sensory information and how those representations contribute to perception, decisions, and actions.

Selected research

02 / Work

A selection of my research experience, spanning auditory neural coding, neural recordings, and behavioral experiments.

01

Neural coding of vocalizations in background noise

Investigated how different background noise types affect neural encoding of vocalizations in the mouse inferior colliculus. Using single-unit recordings and neural response analyses, I examined how neural signal extraction varies with acoustic context and frequency tuning.

Auditory neuroscienceInferior colliculusNeural coding
02

Awake neural recordings & spike sorting

Recorded neural activity in awake mice using Neuropixels probes and established a Kilosort-based workflow for spike sorting and manual curation.

NeuropixelsKilosortElectrophysiology
03

Auditory behavioral experiments

Independently designed and implemented a MonkeyLogic-based Go/No-Go tone-discrimination task, built the behavioral apparatus, and assessed discrimination performance in mice during locomotion and rest.
